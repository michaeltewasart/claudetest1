Thailand

Massaman: the curry shaped by centuries of trade

Cardamom, cinnamon and peanuts tell the story of merchants who crossed oceans.

By Pim Chaiyasit9 min read

A curry unlike the others

Most Thai curries are built on fresh herbs: lemongrass, galangal, chiles. Massaman adds something else, dried spices like cardamom, cinnamon, cloves and nutmeg that feel closer to the Middle East or South Asia.

Those flavors reflect centuries of trade with Persian, Indian and Malay merchants, who brought spices and cooking ideas to the region.

Slow and gentle

Massaman is usually made with beef, chicken or lamb that simmers slowly in coconut milk with potatoes, onions and roasted peanuts until everything is tender.

The result is milder and sweeter than many Thai curries, with a gentle tang from tamarind and a rich warmth from the spices.

Eating it right

Serve massaman with plenty of steamed jasmine rice to soak up the sauce. Many cooks say it tastes even better the next day, once the spices have had time to settle.

A crisp cucumber relish on the side cuts through the richness.

Massaman shortcuts

  • Fry store-bought massaman paste in a little coconut cream until fragrant
  • Add meat, potatoes, coconut milk and a handful of peanuts
  • Balance with fish sauce, palm sugar and tamarind
  • Simmer gently until the potatoes are soft
