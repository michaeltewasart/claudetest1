Ghana & Nigeria

The friendliest rivalry in West Africa is about rice

Smoky, tomato-rich and fiercely debated. We tasted both sides so you don't have to pick.

By Ama Mensah10 min read

One dish, many opinions

Jollof rice is cooked across West Africa, but nowhere is it argued about more lovingly than between Ghana and Nigeria. Ask anyone from either country which version is best and you'll get a confident answer.

At its heart, jollof is rice cooked in a rich base of tomatoes, peppers, onions and spices, so that every grain absorbs the flavor.

Spotting the differences

In our tastings, Nigerian cooks often reached for long-grain parboiled rice and celebrated the smoky 'party jollof' flavor that comes from letting the bottom of the pot catch slightly over a wood fire.

Ghanaian cooks more often chose fragrant basmati or jasmine rice and leaned on aromatic spices. Both sides insisted their way was the original.

The verdict

We refuse to choose. Every pot we tasted was made with pride, and that's what matters most at a wedding, a birthday or a Sunday lunch.

If you're ever offered the crispy bits from the bottom of the pot, say yes. That's where the best flavor hides.

Tips for great jollof

  • Blend tomatoes, red peppers, scotch bonnet and onion into a smooth base
  • Fry the base in oil until it darkens and loses its raw taste
  • Rinse the rice well and cook it gently with a tight lid
  • Don't stir too often, or the grains will turn mushy
