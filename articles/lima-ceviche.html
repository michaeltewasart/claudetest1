Lima's lunchtime ritual: ceviche before 3 p.m.

Why locals insist raw fish is a midday food, and what "tiger's milk" really is.

By Diego Salazar5 min read

A lunchtime dish

In Lima, cevicherías are busiest around noon. Many locals see ceviche as a daytime food, best made with fish bought that morning and eaten while it's at its freshest.

By late afternoon, many of the classic spots are already closing their kitchens.

Lime, chile and salt

Peruvian ceviche is beautifully simple: cubes of firm white fish, freshly squeezed lime juice, salt, thinly sliced red onion and ají limo chile. The acid firms the outside of the fish while the center stays tender.

Modern Lima cooks mix everything just moments before serving, so the fish stays bright and fresh.

Tiger's milk

The tangy, spicy liquid left in the bowl is called leche de tigre, or tiger's milk. It's often served in a small glass alongside the ceviche, and many people swear it's the best part.

On the side, you'll usually find sweet potato and large kernels of Andean corn to balance the heat and acidity.

Ceviche safety tips

  • Use only very fresh, sushi-grade fish from a trusted fishmonger
  • Keep fish and bowls cold until the moment you mix
  • Squeeze limes fresh, and don't over-squeeze or it turns bitter
  • Serve immediately; ceviche doesn't wait well
