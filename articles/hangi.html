Cooking underground: a hāngī from start to finish

Hot stones, a pit in the earth and a meal that brings a whole community together.

By Aroha Ngata11 min read

A Māori tradition

A hāngī is a traditional Māori way of cooking food in an earth oven. It's usually prepared for big gatherings like birthdays, weddings and community celebrations, because it can feed a crowd.

Making one is a shared effort, and the work starts early in the morning.

Fire, stones and earth

First, a pit is dug and a large fire is lit to heat volcanic stones until they glow. The hot stones are placed in the pit, and baskets of food are lowered on top.

The food is covered with damp cloths and then earth, sealing in the heat and steam. Then everyone waits, often for three hours or more.

The reveal

Uncovering the hāngī is the best moment. The food comes out tender, smoky and earthy: pork, chicken, lamb, kūmara (sweet potato), pumpkin, potatoes and stuffing.

Everyone helps serve, and the meal is as much about being together as it is about the food.

What goes in a hāngī

  • Pork, chicken and lamb, cut into large pieces
  • Kūmara, potatoes and pumpkin
  • Bread stuffing wrapped in cabbage leaves or foil
  • Plenty of friends and family to share it with
