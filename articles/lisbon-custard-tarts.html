Portugal

Chasing the perfect custard tart through Lisbon

Blistered tops, shattering pastry and a dusting of cinnamon at every bakery we tried.

By Inês Duarte6 min read

A pastry with a past

Pastéis de nata are often linked to monasteries in Lisbon's Belém district, where egg yolks left over from other uses found their way into sweet recipes.

Today, you'll find them in nearly every café in Portugal, usually still warm from the oven.

What makes a great one

A great pastel de nata has a crisp, flaky shell made from many thin layers of pastry. The custard should be creamy and just set, with dark, caramelized blisters on top from a very hot oven.

We tried tarts at a dozen bakeries in three days. The best ones were all served fresh, within minutes of baking.

How to eat it

Dust it with cinnamon, powdered sugar, or both. Pair it with a bica, Lisbon's strong little espresso.

Eat it standing at the counter, the way the locals do, and try not to order a second. You will anyway.

Baking tips

  • Use store-bought puff pastry rolled into a tight log
  • Press slices into a muffin tin, working the pastry thin up the sides
  • Bake as hot as your oven goes for those dark blisters
  • Let them cool a few minutes so the custard sets
