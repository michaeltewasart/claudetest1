The 18-hour broth: inside a Fukuoka ramen counter

Pork bones, a rolling boil and a chef who hasn't taken a day off in nine years.

By Kenji Mori8 min read

Eight seats and a steaming pot

The shop has eight stools, one menu item and a pot that never seems to stop bubbling. Fukuoka, on the island of Kyushu, is the home of tonkotsu ramen, and here the broth is the whole story.

The chef arrives before dawn to skim, stir and add fresh bones. By the time the doors open at noon, the soup has been boiling hard for most of a day.

Why it turns white

Tonkotsu gets its creamy, almost milky look from a vigorous boil. As pork bones break down, collagen and fat emulsify into the water, creating a rich broth that clings to every noodle.

It is the opposite of a clear consommé. Here, the goal is body: a soup that feels silky on the lips and coats the back of a spoon.

Order like a local

Fukuoka ramen uses thin, straight noodles that cook in seconds. Regulars tell the cook how firm they want them, from soft to barely-cooked.

When the noodles run out but the broth doesn't, ask for kaedama, a second serving of noodles dropped straight into your bowl.

Ramen toppings to try

  • Thin slices of braised pork belly (chashu)
  • A soft-boiled egg marinated in soy and mirin
  • Pickled red ginger and toasted sesame seeds
  • Chopped spring onion and a few drops of garlic oil
