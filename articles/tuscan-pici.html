Pici, garlic and patience in the Tuscan hills

A thick, hand-rolled pasta that needs nothing more than flour, water and a good afternoon.

By Giulia Marchetti6 min read

The pasta of southern Tuscany

Around Siena and Montepulciano, pici is the everyday pasta. It looks like a fat, uneven spaghetti, and that unevenness is the point: every strand is rolled by hand.

Traditional pici dough often skips eggs entirely. It is just flour, water, a pinch of salt and sometimes a splash of olive oil.

Rolling lessons

Nonna Franca cuts the dough into strips and rolls each one under her palms on a wooden board, stretching it outward until it is about as thick as a pencil. Her strands come out perfectly even. Ours do not.

"It doesn't matter," she laughs. "The sauce doesn't care what it looks like."

All'aglione

The classic pairing is all'aglione: a slow-cooked tomato sauce flavored with a mild, oversized local garlic. It cooks gently until the garlic melts into the tomatoes.

Another favorite is briciole, toasted breadcrumbs fried in olive oil, a reminder that this was always a cuisine of making the most of very little.

Basic pici dough

  • 300 g flour, about 150 ml warm water, a pinch of salt
  • Knead for 10 minutes until smooth, then rest 30 minutes
  • Roll thin strips into long ropes under your palms
  • Boil in salted water for 4 to 6 minutes
