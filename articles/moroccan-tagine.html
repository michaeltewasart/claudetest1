Morocco

Slow food, literally: the Moroccan tagine

A conical clay pot, preserved lemons and the gentlest heat you can manage.

By Youssef Amrani8 min read

The pot and the dish

In Morocco, the word tagine means both the clay pot and the stew cooked inside it. The tall, cone-shaped lid traps steam, which rises, cools and drips back down to keep everything moist.

That's why tagines need so little water and can cook for hours without drying out.

Sweet, salty, fragrant

Classic combinations include lamb with prunes and almonds, chicken with preserved lemons and olives, and vegetable tagines heavy with chickpeas and squash.

The spice blend often includes ginger, cumin, turmeric, cinnamon and saffron. Many cooks use ras el hanout, a mix whose name means 'head of the shop', or the spice seller's best.

Low and slow

Tagines are traditionally cooked over charcoal at very low heat. At home, a heat diffuser on the stove does the same job.

Serve straight from the pot with crusty bread to scoop up the sauce. Forks are optional.

Chicken tagine essentials

  • Chicken thighs, onion, garlic and ginger
  • Preserved lemon and green olives
  • Turmeric, cumin, cinnamon and a pinch of saffron
  • Fresh cilantro and parsley to finish
